A note on materials

Each component of Word‑Mark has been meticulously chosen. Bookmarks and notes pages are printed on 100 lb premium cardstock, chosen for its balance of weight and flexibility — sturdy for regular use, light against a hardback spine, and rewarding to write on. The classic 130 lb black cover, finished in gold ink, protects what's inside while earning its place on your bookshelf. Brass bindings are made to be assembled and reassembled as your book of new words grows. Packaging is made from PLA, a plant-based material that cuts greenhouse emissions by up to 70% compared to regular plastic packaging.

Word‑Mark companion app, for those who like that sort of thing

After the book is set down

The Word‑Mark app is free and optional. It’s designed to complement your bookmark, helping you expand your active vocabulary during those quiet moments when you’re not reading.

Currently available only on iPhone.

Scan

Hold your phone over a full bookmark. Scan the front, then the back, and leave no word behind.

  • Only your handwriting is read. The printed title, quote, and margin notes are skipped.
  • Unrecognized words are tagged so you can fix or keep them.
  • Correct any misreads, or swipe left to leave a word out, then save.

Words

Your collected words appear in a list. Hear how each is said, read its history, and see your learning progress at a glance.

  • Search by word or by definition, or slide along the alphabet at the edge to jump to a letter.
  • Add words of your own, and share one word or several.
  • Each word’s page also shows the bookmark quote it was collected from.
  • Words are tagged New, Learning, or Known. Filter by tag, or sort by what needs practice.
  • Practise any word from its own page.

Collection

Collect portraits of the writers quoted on your bookmarks. See on a map where in the world your words originate, and on a calendar when you collected and learned each one.

  • Scanning a bookmark adds the portrait of the writer quoted on it.
  • Portraits come in sets, each showing how many of its writers you’ve collected.
  • Tap a writer to read about them and see the words they helped you collect.
  • The Atlas also names your oldest word and the century it entered English.
  • Tap a day in the Almanac to see its words, with a tick on any that became known.

Learn

Take the words on your bookmark a step further. Play three short games, then use each word in a sentence of your own to make it part of your active vocabulary.

  • Start with a word from your own bookmarks, or take the new word of the day.
  • Three short games get you more familiar with it, and a sentence of your own is the final test.
  • Get one wrong and it comes back sooner.
  • Once you know a word, it returns now and then for a quick visit, so it stays yours.
Quiet by design

Word‑Mark doesn’t send notifications. Nor do we employ psychological tricks, like streaks or red dots, to compete for your precious attention.

The only reminder is the bookmark in your book, which patiently awaits you.

  • You can only grow

    Nothing you learn is ever taken away.

  • Absence costs nothing

    No streaks to protect and no missed days to make up.

  • Depth over frequency

    Rewards go to the words you truly know, however often you open the app.
